4. From forecast to decision

Replace an engine once its P10 remaining-life bound falls to a threshold. Costs are assumptions: a planned replacement costs 1 unit and an in-service failure costs 10 units (change the ratio in the Streamlit app). Simulated on out-of-fold predictions for the 100 training engines. At the cheapest threshold (15 cycles) the rule gives 0 failures and uses 88% of engine life, costing 0.0055 per cycle against 0.0079 for the best fixed-age replacement and 0.0485 for run-to-failure. The threshold was chosen on the same simulation it is scored on, so the gain is optimistic.

Data: NASA Prognostics Center of Excellence, C-MAPSS turbofan degradation simulation (FD001, single operating condition, single fault mode). Intervals cover 73% of held-out engines against an 80% target, so they are somewhat too narrow.
